# Early-years rooms: five different children’s interests

An additional specialist agent reviewed the early-years scenes through a child-development and play-environment lens. The main finding was that a single sun/sea palette, identical drawings and repeated building materials still made the rooms feel like scaled copies. This pass gives each floor plan an authored child-interest portrait.

| Default room | Portrait | Shared-play settings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cozy / apartment | Little noticer: shapes, animals and familiar books | One child plus a grown-up |
| House | Busy helper: market, household routines and pretend meals | Two children |
| Spacious | Little inventor: bridges, vehicles and trying alternatives | Two builders |
| Premium | Story makers: characters, little places and picture sequences | Three friends |
| Executive | Nature & rhythm: leaves, wooden sounds and patterns | Four friends |

These are illustrative portraits, not age/ability rules tied to bedroom size. Every room has one resident child. Additional places indicate visiting friends, rather than additional resident children or beds.

## Visible changes

Each portrait has its own wall/dado/feature palette, coordinated quilt/nook/canopy colours, a woven interest mat and original large posters. The three clipped drawings use different illustration variants. Picture books keep their varied existing covers instead of repeating the interest poster everywhere.

The upper toy shelf now contains a curated nature, pretend-tea, construction, character or wooden-sound set. Morning invitations differ by interest. Shared activity tables have separate coloured work areas and materials for the actual participant count. The three/four-place rooms add one/two grounded stools. The helper’s market has two pretend cups for shopper/helper turns.

Each portrait also has five different activity descriptions. Evening/bedtime retain the clear-floor and put-away routines. The existing pearl-white desk and owner-edited transforms remain available.
