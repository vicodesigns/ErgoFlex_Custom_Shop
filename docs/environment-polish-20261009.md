  lifestyle environment. Existing textured GLB materials remain intact; selected
  untextured seating and plant materials get instance-owned, room-specific
  colors. The source asset cache is never recolored.
- Per-room indirect fill and rim balance across all five dayparts. Daytime fill
  is slightly more neutral, with restrained evening/night fill so practical
  lamps and studio RGB lighting retain their separation.
- Lower-value default desk LED colors in Office and Library at evening/night,
  keeping the Black Birch desktop visible under colored light. Home office's
  overnight practical and desk glow are less intense. Saved custom Groove light
  settings still take precedence over these authored defaults.
- Original procedural printed notebook pages/covers for the lifestyle rooms:
  session scores in Music, project notes in Office, workout logs in Home gym,
  color studies for Artist/Gallery, and diagrams for Workshop/Gaming/Sci-fi.
  Existing book footprints and supporting furniture are retained. Music gets
  a small metronome and pencil; Office gets sticky notes and a pencil.
- All six Educational stages have softer, distance-attenuated sunset pools,
  subdued nighttime window wash, and neutral low-value warm desk LEDs.
- Security, Police and Government floor sun patches fade before the room's
  cutaway edges. Overnight screen emission and desk tint are more restrained.

## Implementation

`room-polish.mjs` owns lifestyle balances and instance material customization;
`room-activity-print.mjs` generates the original canvas print textures;
`room-life.mjs` mounts them on the existing activity books. Group-specific light
changes are in `institutional-education.mjs` and `institutional-government.mjs`.
All owned materials/textures follow the existing disposal path.

The wide-desk test exposed a boundary case when a compact room defaults to 48
inches and a visitor selects 60 inches afterward. Resizing now retains the
current position/yaw and fits an overhanging edge to the existing Groove wall
clearance, rather than leaving the wider top just outside the routing margin.
Stored Groove destinations and collision thresholds are unchanged.

Root Studio and product viewer imports use `environment-polish-20261009` so
visitors receive the same current module graph. Historical upload packages are
not regenerated by this local visual pass.

## Light by the hour in the living and working rooms (November 11, 2026)

A pass over the study, office, kitchen, lounge, bedroom, gallery, coworking space and orbital deck, by hour, after
they got their people. These rooms already change their light with the daypart; what was wrong or missing:

- **Bedroom, weekend hour.** The hour is "a slow morning" with sun on the floor, but the window showed the night
  sky the other rooms' parties have. It now shows the morning view.
- **Gallery at night.** Closed, yet its track lights washed the walls almost as by day. The wash is down from .68 to
  .3 (ambient .12, exposure 1.06): the displays are softly lit and the room is dim.
- **Office at night.** Closed, yet every station's task light was on. One lamp is left on, with the desk's LEDs and
  the city outside (practical .22, wash .2, lamp .6, ambient .12, exposure 1.08).
- **Office, study and kitchen by day.** No daylight came through their windows onto the floor (the lounge, bedroom,
  gallery and coworking space had it). They now have the patch the panes let in: long and low in the morning,
  short after lunch, long and orange in the evening, none after dark.

The patch is `room-window-sun.mjs` (`windowSun(parent, { x, inward, from, to, panes, span })`, `set(daypart, accent)`),
one helper for a window in either side wall; its slant follows the sun the shared light rig uses for shadows
(`room-refinement.mjs`), its reach stops short of the far wall in a narrow layout, and a room can pass its own table
of hours. The lounge, bedroom, gallery and coworking space keep the patches they were built with.

Materials, looked at from eye level in every room: one thing stood out. The orbital deck's bulkhead ribs by the airlock
are a stock model in a toy palette (white, yellow, blue) that read as an unfinished slab beside the detailed hull. In
this bay they take the hull's finish (blue-grey composite, the bands kept as darker amber and navy), on a copy of the
model's material, once the room's props have loaded (`scifi-room.mjs`). The lounge's clouded walls are its limewash
plaster and stay.

Looked at and left as they are: the lounge, coworking space and orbital deck already differ clearly between
all five hours.
